Your AI cites the page. Footing actually reads it.

Footing checks that every link in an AI answer says what its sentence claims, and shows you the page's own words.

Gemini Nano on your laptop reads the best-matching passages of each readable cited page, 1,699 ms median per citation in our bench (M1 Max), and marks the ones with no supporting passage itself. Every “supports” goes to the cloud for the final word, and code refuses any quote that is not word for word in the source.

Questions a judge would ask.

01No Nano on this laptop. What do I still get?+

The whole check, in the cloud, plus a meter of how many citations a Nano laptop would read on-device first. To get Nano: in chrome://flags enable “Gemma 4 for Built-in AI” and the Prompt API, and keep 22 GB free.

02Does Footing tell me whether a claim is true?+

No. It checks whether the cited page says the sentence. A true sentence with the wrong link gets flagged; a false one its source repeats does not.

03How accurate is it, really?+

On twenty claims labelled before any run: Nano's yes/no triage 13 of 20, the cloud's three-way verdict 14 of 20. So Nano never has the last word on “supports”, and every verdict shows the page's own words.

04What leaves my laptop?+

The cited URL goes to this site's fetch proxy, which keeps no copy. A cloud check sends the sentence and its matching passages to the cloud model; a check Nano settles alone sends no text anywhere.

05What happens when the free cloud tier is busy?+

Each answer gets up to 10 citations checked in the cloud automatically; the rest wait for a click. A rate-limited call retries by itself after 15 s, then 30 s; if it still fails, its citation says so: click it again.
